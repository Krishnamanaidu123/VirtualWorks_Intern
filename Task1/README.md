## 🛡️ What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, applications, and data from digital attacks, unauthorized access, damage, or theft. Its primary goal is to ensure the safety and resilience of information technology (IT) infrastructure in the face of evolving threats.

### Key Domains
- **Network Security:** Protecting the integrity of networks and data.
- **Application Security:** Keeping software and devices free of threats.
- **Information Security:** Protecting the privacy and integrity of data at rest and in transit.
- **Operational Security:** The processes and decisions for handling and protecting data assets.

---

## 🔒 The CIA Triad

The **CIA Triad** is the cornerstone model for information security policy. It stands for Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.

| Component | Definition | Practical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Confidentiality** | Ensuring that sensitive information is accessible only to authorized users. | **Banking:** A user's account balance and transaction history are visible only to the account holder and bank personnel. **Implementation:** Encryption, access control lists (ACLs), and multi-factor authentication (MFA). |
| **Integrity** | Ensuring that information is accurate and trustworthy, and has not been altered without authorization. | **Healthcare:** A patient's medical records must remain unaltered to ensure correct diagnosis and treatment. **Implementation:** Hashing (e.g., SHA-256), digital signatures, and file integrity monitoring. |
| **Availability** | Ensuring that systems and data are accessible to authorized users whenever they need them. | **E-Commerce:** An online store's website must be available 24/7 for customers to make purchases. **Implementation:** Redundant systems, failover clusters, and DDoS protection. |

## 👨‍💻 Hacker Types & Ethical Hacking

### What is Ethical Hacking?
Ethical hacking is the **authorized** practice of bypassing system security to identify potential data breaches and threats in a network. Ethical hackers (White Hats) use the same tools, techniques, and processes as malicious hackers to find and fix vulnerabilities before they can be exploited.

### Classification of Hackers

| Hacker Type | Color Hat | Description | Motivation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **White Hat** | 🟢 | Authorized professionals who probe systems for vulnerabilities and fix them. | Security improvement, compliance, and financial gain (legitimate). |
| **Black Hat** | 🔴 | Malicious actors who break into systems for personal gain, theft, or disruption. | Financial gain, espionage, or revenge. |
| **Gray Hat** | 🟡 | Finds vulnerabilities without authorization but discloses them to the owner, often for a reward, rather than using them maliciously. | Personal curiosity or a sense of justice (though methods are illegal). |
| **Script Kiddie** | ⚪ | Inexperienced individuals who use pre-existing scripts and tools to launch attacks. | Notoriety, "cool factor," or minimal skill. |
| **Hacktivist** | 🟣 | Hacks to promote a political, social, or ideological agenda. | Protest, freedom of information, or activism. |

## 🦠 Common Cybersecurity Threats

### 1. Malware
**Malware** (Malicious Software) is any program or file designed to cause harm to a computer, network, or server.
- **Types:**
    - **Viruses:** Self-replicating programs that attach to clean files.
    - **Worms:** Self-replicating malware that spreads without human interaction.
    - **Trojans:** Malware disguised as legitimate software to gain access.
    - **Spyware:** Software that secretly monitors user activity.

### 2. Phishing
A **social engineering** attack where attackers impersonate a trusted entity to trick victims into providing sensitive data (e.g., passwords, credit card numbers).
- **Common Vectors:** Email, SMS (Smishing), Voice (Vishing), and fake websites.
- **Why it works:** It exploits human psychology, creating a sense of urgency or fear.

### 3. Ransomware
A specific type of **extortion malware** that encrypts the victim's files or locks the user out of their system. The attacker then demands a ransom (usually cryptocurrency) to restore access.

## 💥 Impact & Consequences

| Threat | Potential Impact |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Malware** | Data theft, system downtime, operational disruption, and loss of intellectual property. |
| **Phishing** | Identity theft, financial fraud, account compromise, and unauthorized access to corporate networks. |
| **Ransomware** | Complete paralysis of business operations, significant financial loss (ransom + recovery), and reputational damage. |

A robust security posture is built on understanding the fundamentals. The **CIA Triad** provides the necessary framework to design secure systems, while knowledge of different **hacker types** helps identify and mitigate threats effectively. Recognizing common threats like **malware, phishing, and ransomware** is essential for proactive defense. By understanding these principles, organizations can move from a reactive state to a resilient, proactive security stance.
